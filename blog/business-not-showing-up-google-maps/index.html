Business Not Showing Up on Google Maps? Here's Why

October 1, 2026 · By JD Aitken · 5 min read

You searched "roofer near me" from your truck in the shop driveway, and there you were. Then a customer in Orleans says they couldn't find you. Both things are true. When a business isn't showing up on Google Maps, the listing usually exists. It just isn't showing up where your customers are searching from.

That changes what you fix. A missing listing is a setup problem. A listing that ranks in one neighbourhood and vanishes in the next is a ranking problem, and the fixes are different.

First, Check Whether Google Can Show You at All

Search your exact business name on Google Maps. If nothing comes up, start here:

  • The profile isn't verified. An unverified profile doesn't appear to the public. Google's verification guide covers the options.
  • The profile is suspended. Sign in to your Business Profile and look for a warning at the top. Google lists the causes and the appeal steps in its help page on suspended profiles.

A frequent cause of suspension is a business name with extra keywords added, like "Smith Plumbing Ottawa Best Emergency Plumber." It can lift you for a while. Google's guidelines for representing your business ban it, and a competitor can report it. Use the name on your truck and your invoices.

If your name search does show the listing, the profile is fine. Keep reading.

Why Your Business Isn't Showing Up on Google Maps for Customers

Google ranks map results on three things, which it calls relevance, distance, and prominence. Distance is the one that fools owners.

Distance is measured from the person searching. Search from your own driveway and you're the closest business in the city, so of course you rank. A homeowner in Barrhaven searching the same words is 20 kilometres from you and a few minutes from three other companies. They see those three.

Your phone also knows things about you. It's signed in to your Google account, you've opened your own website on it more times than any customer has, and it searches from the same spot every morning. None of that is true for a stranger.

So "I checked and I'm there" is not a check.

How to See What Customers Actually See

The simple version: text three people who live in different parts of the city. Ask each one to search the service you care about most, the way a customer would ("interlock contractor," "furnace repair"), and send you a screenshot of the map. It takes ten minutes and it's usually eye-opening.

The thorough version is a map ranking scan. It runs one search from a grid of points across the city and colours each point by where you rank there. Green near your shop, red in the suburbs you'd like more work from. It turns "we're not showing up" into a specific answer: which search, which part of town, and how far down.

That's also the honest way to talk about a map ranking. Legendary Landscaping ranks in the top 3 for interlocking and hardscape searches in Ottawa, and top 3 for artificial turf in Orleans specifically. Those are claims about a particular search in a particular area. "We're number one on Google" without that detail usually means someone searched from their own office.

What Moves You Up Once You Know Where You're Missing

Your primary category. Check this first. If you're filed as "Contractor" and customers search "deck builder," you're competing for the wrong search. I covered categories in more detail in how Ottawa contractors get found on Google Business Profile.

Your service area doesn't stretch your ranking. Listing every suburb tells customers where you'll drive. It doesn't put you on the map in each one. Google's service area help page covers how to set it up properly. Reaching farther comes from prominence, plus a website with real pages for the areas and services you want.

Reviews, compared to the three businesses beating you. Look at who ranks above you in the screenshots. If they have 80 reviews and you have 12, you know what the gap is. Steady new reviews do more than a burst once a year.

A website that backs the profile up. Google reads your site to judge relevance. A one-page site that says "quality service, call today" gives it very little to match against a search like "basement waterproofing Nepean." This is most of what local SEO for Ottawa trades actually involves.

You can see how the profile and site work together on Legendary's project page.

Where to Start This Week

Do the name search. If you're missing, fix verification or the suspension before anything else. If you're there, get the three screenshots and compare them to what you see from your driveway.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my business not showing up on Google Maps?

The most common reasons are an unverified or suspended Business Profile, a primary category that doesn't match what customers search, or customers searching from too far away from your business location. If you show up when you search your own business name but not for a service like "plumber near me", the listing exists and the problem is ranking, not visibility.

Why does my business show up for me but not for my customers?

Google ranks map results partly by distance from the person searching. When you search from your shop or your house, you're standing right beside your own listing, so you see a result your customers across town don't. Checking from several locations, or with a map ranking scan, shows the real picture.

How long does it take for a new Google Business Profile to show up on Maps?

A verified profile usually appears when someone searches the business name within a few days. Ranking for service searches like "electrician Kanata" takes longer, and depends on your category, reviews, and how many competitors are close to the searcher.

Does adding a service area help me rank in more places?

Not directly. A service area tells customers where you work, but Google still ranks you largely from your business location. Adding every suburb in the city won't put you in the map results in all of them.

Can I pay Google to rank higher on Google Maps?

Not in the regular map results. Google Ads can show a sponsored listing in Maps, marked as an ad, but the unpaid ranking is earned through the profile itself, your reviews, and your website.

How do I check where my business ranks on Google Maps?

Ask a few people in different parts of the city to search the service you care about and send a screenshot. A more complete option is a map ranking scan, which runs the same search from dozens of points across the city and shows your position at each one.
