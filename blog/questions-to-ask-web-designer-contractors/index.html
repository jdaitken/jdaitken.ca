6 Questions to Ask a Web Designer Before You Hire Them

November 1, 2026 · By JD Aitken · 3 min read

The questions to ask a web designer aren't about colours or fonts. They're about who owns what, and how you'll know the site is making your phone ring. Get clear answers to the six below before you sign, and the portfolio becomes the easy part.

I build websites for Ottawa trades, so yes, I'm one of the people you'd be asking. Ask me these too.

1. Who owns the domain name?

Your domain is your address. If it's registered in the designer's name, they control where it points, and moving away gets complicated fast.

You can check this yourself in a minute. Look up your .ca domain on CIRA's WHOIS lookup. It shows the registrar and the registration dates. Personal details are hidden for individuals, so if you can't tell who holds it, ask the designer to show you the account it lives in.

The answer you want: it's registered to your business, and you can log in to it.

2. If I leave, what do I walk away with?

This one gets skipped because nobody signs up planning to leave. Ask it anyway.

Some designers own the site and rent it to you. Leave, and the site stays with them. You start over with a blank page and a phone number customers can't find online.

Here's how I handle it, so you have something to compare against: when a client leaves, for any reason, they get a free copy of the site and every login. No fee to get your own stuff back. Whatever answer you get, get it in writing.

3. What does the monthly fee actually cover?

"Website management" can mean almost anything. For one company it's hosting and a security plugin. For another it's monthly content, Google profile updates and a report.

Ask for the list. How many updates a month? Who renews the SSL certificate? Who replies to your Google reviews? How fast do they answer an email? I wrote a longer breakdown of what professional website management should include month to month, if you want a checklist to hold a quote up against.

Then ask about the contract. How long is the term, and what does it cost to get out early? A long contract isn't automatically bad. Not knowing what's in it is.

4. How will I know the site is bringing in calls?

This is the one that matters most and gets the vaguest answers.

"Traffic is up" sounds great and pays for nothing. What you want to know is how many people called or filled out the quote form, and roughly where they found you. A designer who builds for trades should track both and show you every month.

For a real example: Legendary Landscaping got 17 quote requests in July 2026, their best month so far. 14 came through the website's form and 3 by phone. That's the level of detail to expect. The Legendary Landscaping case study shows how the site and Google work together to get there.

5. How fast does it load on a phone?

Your customers are looking you up on their phone, often standing in their own driveway staring at the problem. A site that takes forever to load loses them to the next result.

Don't take a speed claim on faith. Run the designer's past work through Google's PageSpeed Insights, which scores a page on real-world mobile performance. It's free and takes about a minute. Check the mobile score, since that's the one your customers live in.

6. What happens with my Google Business Profile?

For a local trades business, the map results often come before the website. Someone searches "interlock contractor near me," sees three businesses on the map, and calls one. Your website backs that up, but the profile gets you seen first.

Ask whether the designer manages the profile, including replying to reviews. If they don't, ask who will. I covered the setup side in how Ottawa contractors get found on Google Business Profile, and the bigger picture of ranking is what local SEO for Ottawa trades is about.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I ask a web designer before hiring them?

Ask who owns the domain, what happens to the site and logins if you leave, and exactly what the monthly fee covers. Then ask how you'll know the site is bringing in calls, how fast it loads on a phone, and whether they handle your Google Business Profile. Clear, specific answers to those tell you more than a portfolio does.

Should my business own its own domain name?

Yes. The domain should be registered in your business's name, not the designer's. You can check who holds a .ca domain with CIRA's free WHOIS lookup. If someone else holds it, ask them to transfer it before you sign anything new.

How much does a website for a contractor cost per month?

It depends on what's included. A managed plan can cover hosting, updates, Google Business Profile management and local SEO, while a hosting-only fee covers keeping the site online and not much else. Compare what each monthly fee actually covers, not just the price.

How do I know if my website is bringing in customers?

Count the calls and quote requests it produces each month, and where they came from. A good designer tracks form submissions and phone calls from the site and reports them to you monthly. Traffic numbers on their own don't tell you whether the phone rang.

Does my web designer need to manage my Google Business Profile?

For a local trades business, it helps a lot. Plenty of local customers find you through the map results before they ever see your website, and the profile and the site work together to rank. If your designer doesn't handle it, make sure someone does.

Can I leave my web designer and keep my website?

That depends entirely on the agreement, which is why you ask before you sign. Some designers hand over the full site and every login when you leave. Others own the site and only rent it to you, so leaving means starting from scratch.
